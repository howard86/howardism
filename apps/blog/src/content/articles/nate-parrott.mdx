## Summary

A product designer at [Anthropic](/articles/anthropic) and the originator of [Claude Design](/articles/claude-design). In fall 2025 he was the **only** product designer on Claude Code for VS Code, working with two engineers; the beta shipped end of September 2025. His first-person account (July 2026) is the wiki's primary source on how the tool started and how it is actually used day to day ([Design by Selection](/articles/design-by-selection)).

## The velocity gap that started it

The origin motive, in his words:

> "[Opus 4.5 arrived] in November, and the Claude Code team started shipping fast and aggressively. The engineers were shipping far more than before, while I was still delivering at the pace I always had. I needed to find a way to catch up."

This is a bottleneck migration *inside one team*, caused by asymmetric AI adoption: a model release accelerated the engineering role and left the design role at its old cadence, making design the constraint. It is the concrete, small-scale version of [Implementation Abundance Inverts Product Work](/articles/implementation-abundance-inverts-product-work) — when implementation gets cheap, the expensive step relocates to whatever hasn't been accelerated — and a reminder that "AI raises team throughput" is not distributed evenly across roles ([Role Averaging, Not Role Elimination](/articles/role-averaging-not-role-elimination)).

## The path to the HTML playground

1. **The failed first attempt.** He treated Claude Code as what it looked like — a text-based terminal tool: copy output into Claude, add screenshots, ask "here's a feature we want to add, why don't you design it?" The results "weren't good."
2. **A month of side-project search.** Not a weekend hack but a sustained side effort "for a month or so."
3. **The reframe.** "Claude is really good with HTML. We think of HTML as the format for websites, but it's also a rich, interactive visual medium: anything you can make in a slide deck, a video file, or a PDF, you can make in a web page." He prompted for HTML and built a split view — chat left, output right. Independent arrival at [HTML as the New Markdown](/articles/html-as-the-new-markdown).
4. **Brand distillation.** "I spent a while distilling the essence of Anthropic's brand (the fonts, colors, assets, and principles our products use) into prompts" so output is brand-compliant by default — the manual predecessor of the design-system feature the product now generates ([Living Design System](/articles/living-design-system)).
5. **Internal prototype → adoption.** Designers picked it up immediately for interactive prototypes: instead of mocking every state and wiring them by hand, "you hand Claude your assets and say: make it work," and every artifact has a shareable link.

## The moment it became a product

> "I first realized how compelling Claude Design was at an idea pitch session during an [Anthropic Labs](/articles/anthropic-labs) team offsite: every person there threw together slides using it, often in the middle of the meeting before their turn to present. That session convinced the Labs team to staff it, and Claude Design went from a side project to a real project."

Two things worth keeping: the staffing decision was made on **observed unprompted use**, not a pitch — the Labs bet-factory model selecting on demonstrated pull; and the use that convinced them (slides, mid-meeting) was *not* the use it was built for (product mockups). That reframing — "one click above product design… visuals whose main job is communication and ideation" — came from users, and Parrott closes by inviting more of it: "take it somewhere we haven't thought of yet."

## Relation to Dan Carey's account

[Carey's](/articles/dan-carey) May 2026 talk describes "a designer on the team, Nate," who "hacked together the first prototype over a weekend while working on a different bet," and credits 99% of the value to the ten weeks of iterating that followed. Parrott's own account is compatible but differently weighted: the weekend prototype sat inside a month-plus of side-project exploration, and the motive was a personal velocity gap rather than a bet-portfolio assignment. Neither contradicts the other; Carey has the org view, Parrott the first-person one. (Carey's is `case-study` from a PM talk; Parrott's is `case-study`, first-person, and closer to the events.)
